Zoe does not wait for a free morning. In the videos she posted later, the day was already full: work during daylight hours, a sociology PhD around the rest. What remained were the in-between minutes: wet hands, a pot on the stove, a walk. She uses those minutes for language study.

On her first YouTube video she says she will talk about learning seven languages, then adds that she does not have the same level in all of them. When she says she can speak a language, she means she can hold at least an intermediate conversation. She is still learning.

The languages she learned as an adult are listed together at different levels. Her channel lists Chinese, French, German, Lebanese Arabic, Italian, Turkish, Persian, and English. The first video's chapters move through Chinese, French, Arabic, Turkish, Farsi, and German. She started them at different ages, for different reasons, and with different amounts of time. The text under her videos does not present a finished method for each language.

A later vlog shows her day. She works during the day, and the leftover pieces of the schedule hold language: washing, cooking, walking. On the day she filmed, the plan was three hours of Arabic in the morning and two hours of Turkish in the evening. Other days the mix changes. Sometimes the morning is two hours of Arabic and one of German; the evening, one hour of Turkish and one of Persian. She says the arrangement is flexible, depending on her energy. When energy is low, she changes the mix: less Arabic, some German, and Persian in the hour not used for Turkish.

In a video about studying with a full-time job she names two books, Brian J. Fogg's *Tiny Habits* and James Clear's *Atomic Habits*, as the source of the idea. The chapter titles include the question "Can you still learn a language if you don't have the time?" Then come the Fogg behavior model, ability, and prompt. In that model, a prompt attaches a small action to something already in the day, and ability is what can still be done on a heavy day.

She also uses longer blocks when she has them: morning hours and evening hours. Three hours of Arabic and two hours of Turkish are long study blocks, not the washing-and-walking minutes. Both get used: short minutes on a working day so a language does not drop out, and longer blocks when energy allows.

The first video is dated November 2021, and she calls it her first on the channel. By then she had already been using several languages long enough to tell the story in them. The vlog came a month later, and the tiny-habits video the following June. In these videos she is a sociology PhD student, working by day, recording how she keeps more than one language in use. Later the channel would grow a founder line and a free guide. These three videos are from before that. She still calls herself a learner.
