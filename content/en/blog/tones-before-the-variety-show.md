She calls herself smolbiskit, and also Emi. The About box is a wave, not a résumé. Hey guys. I do language related videos. The channel sits in Entertainment, not in a school. There is no verified second job in the public text, and so none will be invented. What she did put on the internet, with the bluntness of a title, is this: she learned fluent Mandarin Chinese, and she learned it self-taught.

The first thing, according to the line that search engines pulled from the video, was tones. She says so in a way that is almost parental. Tones are the foundation. Mandarin is not only a pile of characters waiting to be memorized. It is a music that changes the meaning of a syllable when the voice goes up, or down, or dips and comes back. A self-taught learner who starts there is a person who has already been humbled by the language. She did not begin with a tourist phrasebook and hope the music would arrive later. She began with the thing that makes the words distinguishable from one another. Without that, a variety show is only faces.

We do not have, in the checked record, the drill she used for those tones beyond a TikTok she links, a short she made or saved about Chinese tones. The story that can be told is the order, not the metronome. Foundation first. Entertainment after.

The after is television. A second video, posted a few weeks later, has a title that does not hedge: *How I Learned Fluent Mandarin Chinese | Through TV Shows*. In the description she points at a playlist — WayV’s *Dream Plan* variety show — the way another learner might point at a textbook spine. Variety is a good word for what she chose. It is talk, games, the leftover chatter between tasks, the kind of speech that does not sit up straight. A person who has already fought with tones can take that chatter in and begin to hear it as language instead of noise.

She names other doors as well, and she names them specifically enough that they should be left specific. A YouTuber, Kelly Yang. A Chinative playlist. Two podcasts: *Learning Chinese Through Stories*, and one whose title in Chinese is 旅歐三小事. The list is not a flex of resources. It is a map of what a self-taught person actually presses play on when there is no teacher to assign the next page. Stories. A host. A variety show. Another learner or speaker on YouTube. The through-line is voice. She is putting Mandarin into her ears on purpose, in forms that were made for someone to enjoy, not only to study.

There is a FluentU link in the description. The report is clear: she does not say she learned with it. The link is an ad-shaped object. It does not get to become her method. The method that can be written is the one she wrote in titles and in the names of shows.

Self-taught, in her usage, is not a claim that she never heard another human. It is a claim that she did not enroll her Mandarin in an institution she wants to credit. The voices she credits are public: a YouTuber, a playlist, podcasts, a boy-group variety show. That is a modern kind of solitude. She is alone with the language and also crowded with it.

The time shape is implied by the two videos sitting close together, both in late 2025, both returning to the same sentence — how I learned fluent Mandarin — first through the self-taught frame, then through television. She is not, in the public text, narrating a childhood in Beijing or a year in a dorm. She is narrating a practice that can be done wherever a screen is. Tones on a short video. A long variety episode. A podcast in headphones. The repetition is the pressing of play, again, after the episode ends, after the tone drill is over, after the story podcast releases another one.

Hesitation, if we stay honest, is not a quoted diary. It is the fact that she felt the need to make a second video at all. The first title is complete. The second title admits that “self-taught” still needs a picture. The picture she chose is not a desk. It is a show. That choice is a small argument: fluency, for her, grew in the hours that look like leisure if you only see the thumbnail.

What she does not give us, and what will not be invented, is a scene of the first time a line from *Dream Plan* arrived in her mouth without translation. She does not write that scene. She writes the playlist link. The aftertaste is the playlist. A person who learned Mandarin without a classroom, who started with tones because she had to, and who then let a variety show do the long work of making the language ordinary.

Self-taught, held to her own words, is also a list of teachers who will never send a bill: a YouTuber, a story podcast, a European-travel podcast in Chinese, a variety cast talking among themselves. She gathered them. She pressed play. She made a second video because the first title still needed a picture of the hours. The picture is a show. The foundation under the show is a tone. That order is the only plot she gave us, and it is enough.
