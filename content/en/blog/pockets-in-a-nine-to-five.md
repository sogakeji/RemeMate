Mattheos Drivas works a full-time nine-to-five. In *How I Learn Multiple Languages While Working a 9-5* (2026-07-19), the first sentence says so. Over the past few years, he writes, he has learned six languages. He says he does it with consistent pockets of time, fitted into the day he already has. In that video he describes one working day: podcasts at the gym, reading with coffee, dead time used for thinking in the language, flashcards during the workday, and two focused sessions after work, one of them an italki lesson with his Cantonese tutor. The chapters are: pre-work studying, at work, post-work studying.

In *How I Learned 6 Languages by 21* (2025-07-26) he lists the materials he used for each language. His general tips, written for any language: do not worry too much about grammar and alphabets at first; find phrases related to your life and common responses, and practice saying them; listen to music often and try translating songs line by line; watch a lot of content in the target language and write down new words; and stay consistent, a little bit day by day. Then the materials. Spanish: Dreaming Spanish, Spanish After Hours, a Spotify playlist. Cantonese: two YouTube channels he names, Amanda and Brittney, and a book, *Cantonese For Everyone* by Chow Bun Ching. Portuguese: a book whose title begins *Tudo bem*, and another playlist. Mandarin: Shuoshuo, Grace, Rita; the HSK series, he writes, could help a book person, but not him. The video also includes a Greek chapter. The description does not say whether Greek is a home language. The count of six languages is his.

In *How I'd Learn a Language From Scratch in 2026* (2026-08-09) he lists five steps he says he has used to become conversational in six languages: find your why; learn starter phrases; learn the basics with structure; go from learner to speaker with immersion and speaking practice; actually go use it. In that video he spends one day on Vietnamese, a language he has not studied, and writes that he went from zero to a real conversation. That wording is his.

An app he is building, Speakeasy, appears in the descriptions. In his public text he writes: "If you have a job and think you do not have time, you do."
